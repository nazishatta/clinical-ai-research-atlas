# Presentation guide — review and personalize

## Opening (30–45 seconds)
Explain the question: a defined corpus of clinical prediction and decision-support publications, with trustworthiness vocabulary selected by the search. Explain the three modalities: when, what, who. Do not call it a census of all clinical AI.

## Show the data audit (45 seconds)
Show actual retained records, text coverage, and the sparse early years. Explain what was excluded and why metadata types do not establish peer review. State that relevance and topic interpretation require your completed review sheets.

## Demonstrate one connected exploration (2–3 minutes)
Select a period and signal. Show annual counts and clarify their denominator. Show the document-frequency chart, fixed NMF topics, and a representative abstract. Show selected coauthorship and compare the two layouts. Explain that the layout changes while relationships do not.

## Synthesize (45 seconds)
Use only findings you have checked. Distinguish lexical emphasis from correctly implemented calibration or explainability, and collaboration centrality from scientific impact. Mention sampling/indexing limits.

## Likely questions to prepare
- Why does this corpus answer your question? What papers can the query miss?
- Does more retrieved literature mean the field grew?
- What is the difference between a search signal and an NMF topic?
- Why NMF and why the selected topic count? What does stability fail to prove?
- How do you reconstruct abstracts and treat missing ones?
- What exactly is an edge? Why normalize large-team contributions?
- Are communities computed before or after filtering/capping?
- What does the network hide when you show only 60 authors?
- How can someone reproduce the same charts after the database changes?
- Which finding changed after you inspected the raw abstracts?

The final answer to the last question must come from your actual review, not a scripted invention.
